# Research AI Use Policy

**Reservoir Characterization Project, SMSE, Colorado School of Mines**
*Version 1.1, August 2026*

**Governing principle.** You are personally responsible for everything you present, submit, or publish, however it was produced. AI is a tool, like a solver or a plotting library — it does not transfer responsibility.

1. **Understand what you show.** Any result you present to anyone — including in a group meeting — must be one you can explain, defend, and reproduce from your own scripts. No result comes out of a box you can't open.
2. **Preliminary is fine; unexplainable is not.** Half-finished results are welcome in internal meetings if labeled as such. However, a result you cannot explain how it is generated does not go on a slide at all, internal or external. Nothing unverified goes into a talk, paper, or proposal outside the group.
3. **Read the papers you cite.** AI is fine for finding literature. No citation goes in a document unless you have read the source and confirmed it supports your claim.
4. **Own your writing.** Ideas, interpretations, and arguments must be yours. AI may improve grammar, clarity, and format. It may not generate substantive text you then adopt.
5. **Protect our data.** Anything non-public — sponsor, consortium, NDA-covered, or unpublished collaborator data — goes only through the group's Claude Team account. Never a personal or free-tier account.
6. **Never AI a peer review.** No manuscript or proposal under your review goes into any tool, on any plan. Someone else's unpublished draft from outside the group needs their permission first.
7. **Keep it reproducible.** Every result is backed by a script that runs without AI and that you have read line by line. Check units, signs, and conventions.
8. **Disclose.** AI is never an author. Follow the venue's policy. If you used AI beyond spelling and grammar, say so in one sentence. Data figures are never AI-generated.
9. **Protect your own training.** Use AI to go faster at what you understand, not to skip learning what you came here to learn.
10. **When unsure, ask.** Asking is never the violation.
